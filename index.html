About the Lab

A research lab centered on community structures in graph-structured systems.

Graverse is a research lab led by Dr. Gülnaz Borozanlı Ekinci, focusing on the study of community structures in graph-structured systems.

The lab investigates fundamental problems in community detection, including overlapping, dynamic, and uncertain network structures, combining graph-theoretic foundations with modern learning-based approaches.

Our goal is to develop robust, scalable, and theoretically grounded methods for understanding how communities emerge and evolve in complex networks.

Research Vision

Advancing community detection in the age of AI.

Complex systems across science and industry are increasingly represented as graphs, where communities reveal the underlying structure of interactions. However, detecting and understanding these communities remains a fundamental challenge, especially in dynamic, uncertain, and high-dimensional settings.

Graverse aims to advance the theoretical and methodological foundations of community detection by integrating graph theory with modern learning-based approaches. We focus on developing methods that are not only accurate and scalable, but also interpretable and robust across diverse types of networks.

Research Areas

Building a rigorous agenda around communities in complex networks.

Community detection lies at the core of many complex systems. Our research focuses on understanding how communities emerge, evolve, and interact in graph-structured data.

01

Community Detection

Identifying cohesive substructures in complex networks, including overlapping and hierarchical communities.

02

Dynamic and Temporal Networks

Studying how communities emerge, evolve, split, and merge in changing graph environments.

03

Attributed and Heterogeneous Graphs

Analyzing community structure in graphs with node attributes and multiple relation types.

04

Graph Representation Learning

Learning representations that preserve relational patterns and reflect community structure.

Signature Problems

Fundamental questions that define how communities are structured and understood.

We focus on fundamental problems that define how communities are structured and understood in complex networks.

Problem 1

Community Detection under Uncertainty

How can we detect meaningful communities in noisy, incomplete, or partially observed graphs?

Problem 2

Overlapping Community Structures

How can nodes belong to multiple communities, and how can these structures be reliably identified?

Problem 3

Temporal Community Evolution

How do communities form, evolve, split, and merge over time?

Problem 4

Community-Aware Representations

How can we learn embeddings that preserve and reflect community structure?
